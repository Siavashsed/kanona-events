Guiding Principles

What We
Believe About
a Room

Every gathering is a composition. Like music, what is left out determines what is felt. These are the principles that guide every event Kanona Projects builds.

01

Scent is the first impression

Before guests see the room or hear the music, they smell it. A single note of cedar, white tea, or aged wood tells the body where it is before the mind catches up. We design the olfactory layer first.

02

Silence is not empty

The pause before the first note of a string quartet. The quiet between courses when conversation drops to a murmur. These moments carry more weight than the sounds that surround them. We protect them.

03

The guest list is the event

Ten people who have something genuine to offer each other will create a more memorable evening than a hundred who share only an invitation. Curation starts with who is in the room.

04

Darkness earns the light

A room that is uniformly bright is a room without drama. We use deep shadow to make a candle feel like a moment worth remembering. Contrast is the vocabulary of atmosphere.

05

Music is architecture

The playlist is not background. It is structure. We treat the sound environment as a timeline - a score that moves the evening through arrival, peak, and departure with intention and care.

06

Nothing happens twice

The best events are genuinely unrepeatable. The specific combination of people, room, weather, conversation, and moment cannot be recreated. We design for the one occasion, not the template.

Designing for
what remains

I design events as sensory experiences - where the scent of a room, the weight of silence before music begins, and the specific warmth of candlelight are as deliberate as the guest list. Every detail is a choice about what feeling a person carries home.

This work started with a simple observation: most gatherings are organized around logistics rather than experience. The food, the venue, the timing - these receive the attention. The atmosphere, the emotional arc of the evening, the quality of the silence between courses - these are left to chance.

What Kanona Projects Does

We work with people who want a gathering to become a memory. The curious minds who feel the difference between a room that has been thought about and one that has simply been booked. Who notice when music shifts the temperature of a conversation, or when the right scent makes a space feel private even when it is not.

The writing here is an extension of that work - an attempt to describe what makes an evening stay with you, and how the decisions behind it get made. Not a guide, exactly. More like thinking out loud from the inside of the practice.
