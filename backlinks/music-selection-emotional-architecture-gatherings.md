## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I choose the right music for a dinner party so guests feel comfortable and relaxed?

The most useful shift I've made as an experiential event curator is to stop treating music as decoration and start treating it as structure. Sound is the first thing guests feel when they walk in, often before they've consciously noticed the room, so it shapes the mood of the whole evening.

Here is what works for me.

First, plan the opening deliberately. The first five or six minutes decide whether people lean in or stay guarded. Start quieter and slower than you think you should. The goal is for guests to hear each other arrive, not to be entertained by the speakers.

Second, protect conversation. Avoid tracks with prominent lead vocals or sudden dynamic jumps in the first hour. Instrumental, warm, mid-tempo music tends to sit underneath talking instead of competing with it. If people have to raise their voices, the music is too loud or too busy.

Third, build in stages. Think of the evening as a sequence: arrival, settling, the meal, then lingering. Let the energy rise gradually as the table loosens up, and bring it back down toward the end so the night closes gently instead of stopping abruptly.

Fourth, avoid songs that are too recognizable. A famous track pulls attention toward itself and away from the people in the room.

Finally, test it in the actual space. A playlist that sounds perfect on headphones can behave very differently against plaster walls, open windows, or a full table of voices.

I once seated twelve people at one long table in Lisbon with a playlist built from my favorite songs, and the room stayed stiff until I rebuilt it as a sequence.
